Notes

Races

Every race gets +2 to one ability score and +1 to another of the player's choice, a size and speed, traits that come from the body, and one signature trait. Each also gains an upgrade at level 7.

Level 7 upgrades

Balance comes from raising everyone, not from cutting what is fun. These are not equal in raw combat power: the Ondri and Sough upgrades are strongest in a fight, while the kenku and human ones matter more in roleplay.

Divinity

Gods and demigods use the same class and level. They differ in kind, not in strength, so a fight never reveals who is which.

Spark

Spark is how much of Voth's power a character holds. You track it on a private card.

  • 0: mortal
  • 1 to 4: demigod (starts at 1)
  • 5 or more: god (starts at 5)

Power must always be accepted or fought. Refused power flows on to those still choosing, and some always gets in.

When a god dies nearby

Each witness chooses in secret.

  • Accept: gain 2 Spark, plus 1 for each witness who resisted successfully. If several accept, they split that extra.
  • Resist: DC 13 Charisma save. Success: gain 1. Failure: gain 2.
  • A demigod's death: every witness gains 1, with no choice.

A demigod who resists every time becomes a god after four god deaths, sooner on a failed save.

What every god-slayer gets

  • Deathless. No aging, eating, sleeping, or breathing. 3e divine rank 0. Dropped its immunities to mind effects and ability damage.
  • Domain gift. Choose a domain. Once per long rest, cast one spell that fits it without a slot, of a level up to half your character level, rounded up. 3e domain powers, converted to 5e slots.
  • Divine vitality. Add half your Constitution score, rounded down, to hit points at level 1. A fighter with 14 Constitution starts at 19. 4e starting hit points, halved.

Demigod only

  • Mortal guise: magic and divine senses read you as mortal.
  • Unbound: enter any god's realm unsensed and ignore its rules.
  • Mortal will: advantage on saves against Spark and against a god's commands.

God only

  • Realm: inside your own realm you sense everything and can reshape terrain and weather. Outside it you get nothing extra. BECMI Immortals and 3e divine realms, cut down.
  • Faith: a pool equal to your Spark. See below.
  • Bound: to act against your domain, make a DC 15 Wisdom save. On a failure you can't. On a success you still lose 1 Faith.
  • Sensed: any god knows when you enter their realm.

Faith

Faith is reach. It lets a god act in places they are not, which matters most in a campaign about politics.

UseCostEffect
ListenFree, or 1You always know when a worshipper prays to you. Spend 1 to see and hear through them for a minute.
Miracle1Cast a spell you know with a worshipper as its origin, at any distance.
Champion2A mortal adds 1d4 to attacks, saves, and checks for a week, and acts for you elsewhere.
CreateVariesIn downtime, make a place, a relic, or a species.

Regain 1 per long rest while your worshippers thrive. Your maximum drops when they are killed or lose faith, so a god has people to defend and enemies have something to attack. 3e divine rank and 5e Theros piety, simplified to one pool.

Ascending and dying

Ascending against your will

At 5 Spark, a demigod's domain binds at the next long rest. They lose the three demigod traits and gain the four god traits. No way back is known.

Death

  • Death is permanent.
  • The dead character's power disperses to whoever killed them.
  • The amount released reveals whether they were a god or a demigod.
  • The player returns as an heir: a follower or creation of the dead character who caught a splinter, at party level.

Levelling

The divine power burned out the heroes' mortal skill. Level measures how much of their shard they have learned to wield. Level up every two sessions, ending around level 7 or 8.

Domains

A domain is the one concept a god-slayer's power attached to: the answer to "god of what?" Gods and demigods both have one, so a domain reveals nothing about tier. It sets the Domain gift's spells, what a god is Bound against, and who a god's natural allies and rivals are.

The domain list is not written yet. It waits until after session negative. The plan is fifteen to twenty broad domains, each with its spells by level and what counts as acting against it, and players may propose their own.

Factions

Where the peoples stand

  • Sough: split among every faction, with many among the Refusers.
  • Corrin: largely split between the Concord and the Faithful.
  • Dragonborn: most of Voth's Faithful.
  • Dural: many of the Reavers.
  • Ondri: courted by every faction to vouch for it, the Concord most of all.
  • Lorn: mixed in among all peoples.

Campaign

The campaign

  • Setting: a new universe at galactic scale, separate from Drakkamor
  • Party: 4 to 6 players, starting at level 1
  • Sessions: 10 to 15, each 2 to 2.5 hours
  • Tone: tragic, dark, political
  • Lethality: characters can die permanently
  • Balance: 55% roleplay, 35% combat, 10% exploration
  • Homebrew: to be decided as we build

Table rules

  • All editions of D&D are in play, converted to 5e math.
  • Where editions disagree, use the version with more fun, options, and freedom.
  • Balance by adding to others before taking fun away.
  • Player expression first, then roleplay, mystery, and consequences.

The road to session one

  1. Session negative. A one-shot. The players are mortal heroes who set out to kill their god, Voth. It ends on a choice: resist the power and become a demigod, or accept it and become a god. Planned in its own chat.
  2. Session 0. The gap years, played. In private, each player describes what they built or where they went. Together, as authors, everyone adds a world, an NPC god, and a rumor about another god-slayer. Planned in its own chat.
  3. The campaign. It opens a few years after the kill, on what the players made, still thriving. Suggested: three to seven years
