To be a god

The dragon god of the universe is dead. Her power now lives in the mortals who killed her, and none of them agree on what to do with it. Somewhere behind them, a prison stands empty.

4 to 6 playersStart at level 110 to 15 sessions of 2 to 2.5 hours 55% roleplay, 35% combat, 10% explorationTragic, dark, politicalDeath is permanent

The two gods

Voth

Gold dragon. God of the universe. Dead.

Killed because mortals were tired of a god running their lives. Her power dispersed into everyone near her when she died.

DM only
She was a good god, not a tyrant. She did interfere too much, but she meant well, and her power was what kept Ado imprisoned. Only the ancient Corrin archivists know the whole truth.

Ado (eh-do)

Phoenix. Voth's opposite. The final threat.

DM only
Released when Voth died. He presents himself as a god of peace, and through peaceful politics he assassinates god after god and topples nation after nation until he is the only god left. He is served by a demigod, who is either a player or an NPC depending on how session negative ends. The players do not know he exists.

Ado's clock

DM only: what happens if nobody interferes
  1. He appears through his servant as a peacemaker.
  2. He brokers the first treaty and gains a seat in the Concord.
  3. Gods who signed his treaties start dying, and rivals are blamed.
  4. The Concord hands him emergency authority.
  5. He is the last god left.

Races

Every race gets +2 to one ability score and +1 to another of the player's choice, a size and speed, traits that come from the body, and one signature trait. Each also gains an upgrade at level 7.

Level 7 upgrades

Balance comes from raising everyone, not from cutting what is fun. These are not equal in raw combat power: the Ondri and Sough upgrades are strongest in a fight, while the kenku and human ones matter more in roleplay.

Divinity

Gods and demigods use the same class and level. They differ in kind, not in strength, so a fight never reveals who is which.

Spark

Spark is how much of Voth's power a character holds. Track it on a private card for each player.

  • 0: mortal
  • 1 to 4: demigod (starts at 1)
  • 5 or more: god (starts at 5)

Power must always be accepted or fought. Refused power flows on to those still choosing, and some always gets in.

When a god dies nearby

Each witness chooses in secret.

  • Accept: gain 2 Spark, plus 1 for each witness who resisted successfully. If several accept, they split that extra.
  • Resist: DC 13 Charisma save. Success: gain 1. Failure: gain 2.
  • A demigod's death: every witness gains 1, with no choice.

A demigod who resists every time becomes a god after four god deaths, sooner on a failed save.

What every god-slayer gets

  • Deathless. No aging, eating, sleeping, or breathing. 3e divine rank 0. Dropped its immunities to mind effects and ability damage.
  • Domain gift. Choose a domain. Once per long rest, cast one spell that fits it without a slot, of a level up to half your character level, rounded up. 3e domain powers, converted to 5e slots.
  • Divine vitality. Add half your Constitution score, rounded down, to hit points at level 1. A fighter with 14 Constitution starts at 19. 4e starting hit points, halved.

Demigod only

  • Mortal guise: magic and divine senses read you as mortal.
  • Unbound: enter any god's realm unsensed and ignore its rules.
  • Mortal will: advantage on saves against Spark and against a god's commands.

God only

  • Realm: inside your own realm you sense everything and can reshape terrain and weather. Outside it you get nothing extra. BECMI Immortals and 3e divine realms, cut down.
  • Faith: a pool equal to your Spark. See below.
  • Bound: to act against your domain, make a DC 15 Wisdom save. On a failure you can't. On a success you still lose 1 Faith.
  • Sensed: any god knows when you enter their realm.

Faith

Faith is reach. It lets a god act in places they are not, which matters most in a campaign about politics.

UseCostEffect
ListenFree, or 1You always know when a worshipper prays to you. Spend 1 to see and hear through them for a minute.

Champion2A mortal adds 1d4 to attacks, saves, and checks for a week, and acts for you elsewhere.

Regain 1 per long rest while your worshippers thrive. Your maximum drops when they are killed or lose faith, so a god has people to defend and enemies have something to attack. 3e divine rank and 5e Theros piety, simplified to one pool.

Ascending and dying

Ascending against your will

At 5 Spark, a demigod's domain binds at the next long rest. They lose the three demigod traits and gain the four god traits. No way back is known.

DM only
The cycle of god-killing can be broken by combining the power of Ado with the power of Voth.

Death

  • Death is permanent.
  • The dead character's power disperses to whoever killed them.
  • The amount released reveals whether they were a god or a demigod.
  • The player returns as an heir: a follower or creation of the dead character who caught a splinter, at party level.

Levelling

The divine power burned out the heroes' mortal skill. Level measures how much of their shard they have learned to wield. Level up every two sessions, ending around level 7 or 8.

Domains

A domain is the one concept a god-slayer's power attached to: the answer to "god of what?" Gods and demigods both have one, so a domain reveals nothing about tier. It sets the Domain gift's spells, what a god is Bound against, and who a god's natural allies and rivals are.

The domain list is not written yet. It waits until after session negative. The plan is fifteen to twenty broad domains, each with its spells by level and what counts as acting against it, and players may propose their own.

Factions

After each session, pick two or three factions the players did not deal with and apply one small move each. Advance a big development about every three sessions. Player actions should replace these freely. Leaders are described by role until the NPC gods are built.

Where the peoples stand

  • Sough: split among every faction, with many among the Refusers.
  • Corrin: largely split between the Concord and the Faithful. The ancient archivists stay out until late in the story.
  • Dragonborn: most of Voth's Faithful.
  • Dural: many of the Reavers.
  • Ondri: courted by every faction to vouch for it, the Concord most of all.
  • Lorn: mixed in among all peoples.
DM only: how the factions connect
  • The dead Maker is Ado's first assassination, and the Reavers get blamed.
  • The Faithful's militant wing, the Unbowed, and the Foundry's weapon all end up killing gods for different reasons. Ado only needs to encourage them.
  • Ado's Lorn work inside the Foundry without knowing whose designs they serve.
  • The Foundry's weapon sends the dead god's power to Ado.

Ideas in reserve

Proposed but not added. More factions will form as the NPC gods are created.

  • The Wake: opportunists who follow dying gods to catch dispersing power, and eventually arrange the deaths.
  • The Penitent: god-slayers who regret killing Voth and will follow anyone who offers absolution.
  • The Tri-Court: Ondri arbiters offering neutral ground where gods can meet. Useful as a recurring location.

Secrets

The truth about Voth
  • She was a good god and the warden holding Ado. She interfered too much, but she meant well and was never tyrannical.
  • A gold dragon who breathed fire. Her four children, her only young, died in the assault that killed her.
  • Her mate, Zandrioch, was taken during the initial imprisonment of Ado. He vanished during that fight without a trace.
  • The party caused Ado's release by killing her.
  • Only the Corrin know her true nature and why she did what she did. The ancient archivists reveal it late in the story.
  • Voth's Faithful revere her and want her power regathered, but hold only fragments of the truth.
Voth's island
  • Reached only by the prayer road, the path prayers take to her. It runs among the stars and between planets, and each step covers thousands of miles. Nobody knew it existed. Only Lorn can see it.
  • At the road's end an island appears, seemingly out of nothing. The last stretch crosses ocean that drops like a curtain into space at the edges.
  • A beach, then jungle, with a clear path to a large mountain at the center. The island is warm and the mountain is hot.
  • The mountain's entrance is the Hall of Asking. Right leads to the roost of Voth and Zandrioch. Left leads to the children's roost. Straight on and deeper is where Voth waited, guarding the Weight.
  • The halls are mostly unornamented. The whole island is simple.
  • Guia is the Lorn who guided the Host there.
Ado
  • A phoenix, the god opposed to Voth, freed when Voth died.
  • Goal: to be the only god left, by posing as a god of peace while gods are assassinated and nations fall.
  • His pitch is what Voth's killers wanted: a god who promises to leave mortals alone. Suggested
  • His servant is a demigod who worships him: a player, offered privately and only if they opt in, or an NPC. Session negative decides.
  • He can sense his Lorn and speak to them in dreams, but cannot control them.
  • He can accept dispersing power from a distance.
Clues that Voth was a warden, not a tyrant
  • The prison stands empty, and its locks were made of Voth's own power.
  • Worlds she "interfered" with are failing without her.
  • The Sough elder fields, which she used to burn back, are swallowing worlds.
  • The Dural, whom she confined, are now conquering.
  • A dragonborn order guarded the prison's location without knowing what it held.
  • No Lorn woken by her grief has been born since she died.
  • The ancient Corrin archivists hold the full record. This arrives late.
Clues that point to Ado
  • Every dead god had recently signed a treaty he brokered.
  • Deaths near his treaties leave less power for the witnesses than they should.
  • Ondri who stood near his servant say the words seemed true, yet their own skin showed dread.
  • Corrin records show Lorn births stopped when Voth died, yet new Lorn keep appearing.
  • New Lorn have orange embers and resist fire, not radiant damage.
  • The Foundry's god-killing weapon leaves no power with its wielder.
  • The dragonborn who guarded the prison are being hunted into silence.
The Lorn's two origins
  • Grief-born: woken by Voth's attention when a star died. Nearly all Lorn alive today.
  • Leak-born: woken occasionally by Ado's power seeping from his prison. Rare, old, and unaware they differ.
  • Since Voth's death: every new Lorn belongs to Ado.
  • Ado's Lorn want to learn just as the others do. The difference is how they use what they learn: they subtly and unwittingly steer technology and development around them according to Ado's designs.
  • Some of the bitter Lorn are leak-born, but not all.
  • Once free, Ado can kill stars on purpose to make more. Suggested
The cycle

Those who killed Voth believed peace, freedom, and prosperity would follow. Instead the killing of gods repeats.

The cycle can be broken by combining the power of Ado with the power of Voth.

Campaign

Your answers

  • Setting: a new universe at galactic scale, separate from Drakkamor
  • Party: 4 to 6 players, starting at level 1
  • Sessions: 10 to 15, each 2 to 2.5 hours
  • Tone: tragic, dark, political
  • Lethality: characters can die permanently
  • Balance: 55% roleplay, 35% combat, 10% exploration
  • Homebrew: to be decided as we build

Table rules

  • All editions of D&D are in play, converted to 5e math.
  • Where editions disagree, use the version with more fun, options, and freedom.
  • Balance by adding to others before taking fun away.
  • Player expression first, then roleplay, mystery, and consequences.

The road to session one

  1. Session negative. A one-shot. The players are mortal heroes who set out to kill their god, Voth. It ends on a choice: resist the power and become a demigod, or accept it and become a god. Planned in its own chat.
  2. Session 0. The gap years, played. In private, each player describes what they built or where they went. Together, as authors, everyone adds a world, an NPC god, and a rumor about another god-slayer. Planned in its own chat.
  3. The campaign. It opens a few years after the kill, on what the players made, still thriving. Suggested: three to seven years
  4. The end. The scheming among new gods is most of the campaign. Ado is the threat that surfaces toward the end.

Tone notes

  • Let the players enjoy godhood first. Tragedy needs something to lose.
  • Darkness should come from the players' choices and their costs.

    How to build a creature

    1. Niche. What did this people do, or survive, in Voth's universe?
    2. Anchor. One familiar body plan. It need not be an animal.
    3. Twist. One alien thing that is not anatomy: how they are born, die, sense, or speak.
    4. Consequence. What the twist does to their society.
    5. Signature trait. One mechanical hook the table remembers.

    Extra creature ideas can live as NPC peoples with a paragraph of lore and no balanced traits.
